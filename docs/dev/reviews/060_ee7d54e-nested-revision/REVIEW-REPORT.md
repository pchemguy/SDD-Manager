## Finding records

### R-001 — “Supporting owner” does not describe its column

**Location:** lines 32–38. **Confidence:** high.

The third column contains an agreement check (“Metadata and entry agree…”), procedure categories, and evidence categories (“actual packaging and client evidence”). These are not comparable owners. The table therefore mixes what to assess, where detail might reside and how a claim is verified, while presenting them as one relationship. It also suggests supporting procedures as a default even though an entry may validly contain its own procedure.

**Proposal:** Prefer two columns, concern and review criterion, integrating only necessary ownership distinctions. If a third column is retained, give it a precise job and make every cell satisfy that job. Do not require reference extraction merely to fill the table. **Recheck:** every cell answers its header; no additional module requirement appears.

### R-002 — Child-reference sentence exceeds the selected inventory scope

**Location:** line 50, last sentence; compare lines 42 and 52. **Confidence:** high for inconsistent scope, without inferring why the human removed the earlier qualifier.

“A child reference’s local link does not replace its entry-level route” imposes entry-level routing for child references without limiting that claim to `references/*.md`. The entry-routing inventory was explicitly narrowed to that pattern and the nested-module qualification removed. The surviving sentence leaves a separate recursive requirement in place. This ambiguity is consequential even though the approved routing paragraph itself is clear.

**Proposal:** Remove the sentence, or limit it expressly to files in the selected inventory. Preserve every-file coverage and entry-only selection for `references/*.md`; do not silently broaden or weaken that policy. **Recheck:** the paragraph, inventory check and local onward-routing criterion agree on scope. A nested-recursion policy would require a separate decision.

### R-003 — Two reference “contrasts” do not contrast comparable conditions

**Location:** lines 74–75; section title at 69. **Confidence:** high.

“Coherent reference versus neighboring or child-module responsibility” compares a quality judgment with a responsibility category. A coherent reference can have a neighboring owner; neither is an alternative test condition. “Procedure versus guidance, contract or example” classifies genres rather than contrasting successful and deficient guidance. The intended criteria are useful, but the table does not tell a reviewer what changed condition to assess.

**Proposal:** Either label these rows as role/interface checks, or make genuine contrasts: clear local scope versus overlap with an adjacent owner; role-appropriate organization versus procedure-only rules wrongly applied to explanatory material. Keep contextual-use versus missing-context as a real contrast. **Recheck:** each contrast provides comparable starting conditions and a discriminating expected result; genre coverage remains intact.

### R-004 — Entry-only assessment is insufficiently distinguished from target inspection

**Location:** lines 52 and 81; behavioral evidence qualification at 102. **Confidence:** high for sequencing, moderate for practical consequence.

The text first tells the reviewer to inspect descriptions against their targets, then assess requests using only the entry. An analytical source check can still judge whether the entry contains enough information after reading targets. A consumer trial claiming entry-only selection must withhold those contents. The sequence does not name that distinction, despite correctly separating source review and observed behavior elsewhere.

**Proposal:** Specify source coverage as identifying the selection evidence actually present in the entry. For a selected behavioral trial, evaluate selection in a context containing the entry and ordinary request facts before exposing reference contents; inspect target agreement separately. No trial is made mandatory. **Recheck:** target knowledge cannot be presented as evidence that an entry-only consumer selected correctly.

### R-005 — Repeated routing language creates avoidable edit surfaces

**Location:** lines 20, 30, 50–52, 63, 81 and 96; related ownership repetition at 19, 26 and 65. **Confidence:** high; reduction benefit is editorial, not measured runtime evidence.

Lookup criteria, ordered review steps and scenario tables justify some repetition. However, the inventory comparison and selection-from-entry rule are retold almost completely in the routing subsection and step 3; the reference loading row then combines entry coverage, local trigger agreement, child/resource discovery and chain avoidance. Different jobs share a large repeated instruction set rather than pointing to one complete criterion.

**Proposal:** Keep the full rule under Reference routing coverage. Let the procedure refer to that check briefly, keep the local reference row about agreement with its caller and onward dependencies, and retain the scenario as a genuine contrast. Condense repeated ownership wording only where it adds no local distinction. **Recheck:** every required check remains reachable; the approved routing requirement stays explicit and prominent.

### R-006 — Review questions drift into editing commands and abstract phrasing

**Location:** lines 7, 16–18, 24, 63, 73 and 79. **Confidence:** high for wording; no claim of a demonstrated authority violation.

Questions addressed to a reviewer are interspersed with “Use headings” and “Remove wording,” while repair authority is conditional elsewhere. The intended assessment is inferable, but the voice is inconsistent. “Reading boundary,” “section jobs,” “distribution boundary” and “reviewed entry/reference role” also pack different concepts into shorthand; some are useful technical terms, others obscure a simple action. Paragraph 7 and row 63 each combine several concerns.

**Proposal:** Phrase criteria as assessments or recommendations; reserve editing instructions for accepted revision. Use concrete wording where it clarifies the actor and evidence, such as required previously loaded instructions instead of “reading boundary.” Keep actual packaging-containment terminology where needed. Move dependency-inspection detail from the opening to the procedure if it adds no scope distinction there. **Recheck:** the reviewer can identify what to inspect/report and what requires accepted editing authority; wording stays precise without teaching the standard.
